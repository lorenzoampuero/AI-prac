You can use AI to tailor a cover letter or CV to a specific posting — as long as every claim stays traceable to something you actually did. The workflow has four moves: **gather evidence, map it to the posting, draft inside the truth, and check**. Skip the first move and the model fills the gaps with plausible fiction you will have to defend in an interview.

## Gather evidence before anything else

Write a one-page brag sheet: roles and dates, projects and what you personally did, numbers you can defend, tools you actually used, outcomes with context. This is your [grounding](/glossary/grounding) bank — the only material the model is allowed to draw from. It does not know your history, and an ungrounded draft is a nicer-sounding guess about a stranger.

## Map the posting

Turn the posting into a requirements list, then mark each line:

- **Evidence.** Covered, with the specific proof.
- **Partial.** You touched this. Say how, at the true scale: “familiar with X from Y”, not “expert in X”.
- **Miss.** Not covered. Either leave it alone or address it honestly in one line — never let a bullet silently absorb it.

This map is also your cover letter's outline: requirements on the left, evidence on the right, and the letter argues the strongest three or four pairings.

## The check is reading your own draft

Before sending, read every sentence and point to the evidence behind it. Anything you cannot point to is an [unsupported claim](/glossary/unsupported-claim) — cut it or correct it to the true scale. Two failure patterns to hunt: **scope upgrades** (“led” when you contributed; “owned” when you supported) and **time stretching** (“years of” where the evidence is a project). Both read fine and both are easy for an interviewer to puncture with one follow-up question.

## Transfer

- Interviews: the evidence bank doubles as your prep sheet — every claim has a story behind it.
- Performance reviews: same discipline, internal audience; upgrades age badly when promotions are decided.
- Freelance profiles and bios: map claims to deliverables, cut anything you cannot show.
- LinkedIn summaries: write from the evidence bank, not from the job title you wish you had.
