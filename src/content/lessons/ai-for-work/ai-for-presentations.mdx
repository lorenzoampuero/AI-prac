A deck is an argument, not a document. Most AI slide help fails for one reason: it generates paragraphs and calls them slides. The fix is to use AI where it is genuinely strong — the **spine** of the argument, the outline, the speaker notes — and build the slides like slides.

## Slides are not prose

- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs three sentences to be understood, it is a document wearing a slide costume.
- **The title carries the point.** “Retention is the bottleneck” is a title; “Retention” is a topic.
- **Evidence lives in one visual.** A chart, a diagram, two numbers. Nothing else.
- **Sentences go in the notes.** The slides support you; the notes are what you actually say.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “Make me a 15-slide deck about our Q3.”
>
> You get topic soup — Introduction, Overview, Key Metrics, Conclusion — with paragraphs on every slide. The audience reads ahead, you narrate your own slides, and no decision emerges.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “Audience: the exec team. Decision: approve two support hires. They must believe: backlog growth is unsustainable; temps fix it short-term only; two hires clear it by Q1. Give a ten-slide spine for a 15-minute slot where every title is a full-sentence claim, plus the single piece of evidence each claim needs.”
>
> The output is an argument you can check slide by slide — and it is checkable *before* any design work begins.

## Why it works

The model is a strong structuring partner and a weak visual designer. Decks live or die on their spine: an argument whose titles read as claims works even when skimmed, because the skimmer absorbs the reasoning. AI also drafts speaker notes faster than you will, and — used *after* the spine is fixed — notes reinforce the argument instead of wandering. Keep the build step for yourself, where taste matters; keep the outline step with AI, where structure is cheap to change.

## Transfer

- Status updates: same loop at two-slide scale — decision, claims, evidence.
- Pitches: the “what must they believe” list is the real work; AI just formats it.
- Reviewing a colleague's deck: check whether the titles alone tell the story.
- Speaker notes in your register: feed AI one paragraph of how you actually talk.
