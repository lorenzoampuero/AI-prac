An AI-drafted email fails in exactly two ways: it does not sound like you, and it promises things you never agreed to. Both are fixable with the same structure: **show your voice with examples**, and **bound the facts with placeholders**.

## Voice is examples, not adjectives

"Professional but friendly" describes a thousand different writers. Two emails you actually sent describe one. Paste them as voice samples — the same move as [few-shot examples](/learn/prompting/examples-and-few-shot), applied to yourself: openers, how you phrase requests, how you sign off. The draft inherits your cadence instead of a generic one.

## The four-part request

1. **Outcome** — what the email must achieve: apologize, explain the fix, confirm next steps.
2. **Voice samples** — one or two real emails you have sent to similar people.
3. **Facts and boundaries** — what must be in it (the refund, the correct date) and what must **not** be promised (no discounts, no future credits).
4. **[Placeholders](/glossary/placeholder)** — "where a detail is missing, insert [refund reference] instead of inventing it".

That last rule is the quiet hero. A model asked to "write the email" will happily produce a reference number, an amount and a deadline that you might send by accident. A model asked for placeholders gives you a draft with visible gaps — and gaps you can see are gaps you can fill.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** "Write a professional apology email to a client about a billing mistake."
>
> Generic tone, invented specifics, and a promise about "adjusting charges" you are not authorized to make.

## A better example

> **Ask:** "Write a reply about charging the client for a fourth storage week after their goods shipped — our mistake. It must apologize plainly, state that the week will be refunded, and give a next step. Match the voice of these two emails I sent before: [paste]. Do not promise anything beyond the refund. Where a detail is missing, insert a bracket placeholder like [refund reference]."
>
> Same speed, your tone, and no commitments you did not make.

## Before you send

Read it once as the recipient — a [rubric](/glossary/rubric) pass: does it answer their question, does it promise anything you cannot deliver, is every bracketed placeholder filled in? That is the [five-question check](/learn/prompting/evaluate-before-you-ship) applied to correspondence — thirty seconds, no exceptions for "just an email".

Keep the voice pack current: every few months, replace the samples with more recent emails. Voice drifts — yours included — and two fresh samples cost nothing while keeping the drafts fitted to the person you are now.

## Transfer

- Recurring replies: keep a two-email voice pack ready to paste.
- Sensitive topics: more boundaries, more placeholders, no drafting shortcuts around a human review.
- Any language: samples work wherever you write.
