Every generation samples anew — that is the same property that makes results feel alive, and it is the enemy of a series. If a character, product or style must look the same across many images, consistency has to be engineered: **freeze the description, reuse the mechanics, and edit instead of re-rolling.**

## Why series drift

Each run decides everything fresh from your words. Re-describe your character loosely, in different words each time, and you re-open every decision — fur colour, proportions, style, line weight — for a new roll. Small phrasing changes move big properties. The fix is not “try harder to describe it the same”; it is having a description that is *the same by construction*.

## Mechanics that help

- **Lock the text.** Keep the sheet in a file and paste it verbatim into every prompt. Never paraphrase per image — paraphrasing is re-rolling.
- **Reuse the mechanics your tool offers.** Some tools expose a seed that fixes the starting randomness; reference images and edit chains also carry likeness forward. Use what your tool has — the principle is the same: fewer fresh samples.
- **Edit around the subject.** When a composition is accepted, [edit the scene around the character](/learn/ai-images/iterate-and-edit) instead of regenerating the whole picture.
- **Change one variable per image.** Poses, scenes and props vary; the subject block stays frozen.
- **Audit the series.** Line the images up and check the frozen features one by one. Drift is easiest to catch side by side.

## A bad example

> **Ask (image 1):** “A cute orange fox for our storybook.”
> **Ask (image 3):** “The fox, but in a forest, storybook style.”
>
> Two different cartoons. Every descriptor moved, so every descriptor was re-rolled.

## A better example

> **Ask:** the full sheet — “Milo, a small red fox with a white chest patch, one notched left ear and dark paws; flat vector style, thick cream outlines, muted forest palette, soft even light” — plus “standing in a misty forest clearing.”
>
> The character variables never move; only the scene line changes between images.

## Why it works

Consistency is not a quality you request — it is a set of variables you remove from the lottery. The sheet works because it is identical input, and identical input narrows the sampling space around the same character. The mechanics work because edits and reference-driven flows reuse existing pixels rather than describing them again. The more of the subject you freeze, the smaller the drift between images — and what drift remains, the side-by-side audit catches.

## Transfer

- Brand kits: a product sheet with exact material, colour and light words reused in every asset.
- Comics and storybooks: one character sheet, many scene lines.
- Avatars and profile images: freeze the face description; vary pose and background.
- When drift appears, find which line moved — then re-freeze it.
