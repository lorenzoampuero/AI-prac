Colour decides how an image feels before anyone reads its details. The same room can be calm or chaotic with the same furniture — change the palette. Two or three colour decisions carry most of that weight: **a dominant hue, its temperature and how saturated everything is**. All three are writable.

## Build a palette like an interior designer

- **Dominant** — one hue owns the picture; most of the frame is its family.
- **Support** — one or two related colours carry the rest.
- **Accent** — a single contrasting note, used sparingly, gives the eye somewhere to land.

Name colours the way paint shops do — terracotta, sage, cream, charcoal, deep teal — rather than as “nice colours”. Concrete colour words are claims the model can render; vibes are not.

## Temperature is the mood dial

- **Warm** — amber, rust, gold: energy, nostalgia, comfort.
- **Cool** — blue, slate, teal: calm, distance, technology.
- **Neutral** — greys, beiges, off-whites: quiet and documentary.
- **Mixed** — hot and cold together: tension and energy, best used on purpose.

## Saturation is a volume dial

Muted palettes feel considered and quiet; vivid ones feel loud and energetic. Pastels sit at the calm end: light and low-saturation. Say which one you want — “muted and matte” is a decision, “colourful” is an invitation for chaos.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “A kitchen with perfect colour harmony and lots of colour.”
>
> The model picks its own palette — usually a safe, generic one — and “harmony” cannot be rendered or debugged.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “A kitchen in cream and pale oak with muted sage-green cabinets, one terracotta bowl on the counter, soft morning light.”
>
> Two families, one accent, one saturation level: a palette you can adjust by editing a single word.

## Why it works

Colours are physical properties: a paint has a hue, a temperature and a finish, and [generative systems](/glossary/generative-ai) render those properties literally. A named palette also holds together across the frame — the same few colours recur everywhere, which is what “harmonious” actually means. And because colour sits inside light, the two compound: warm light amplifies warm palettes. Say both when the mood has to be unmistakable.

## Transfer

- Brands: pick the palette once, then reuse the same three colour words in every prompt.
- Interiors and products: muted beats vivid when you want quality cues; vivid beats muted when you want attention.
- Mood boards: vary one dial — temperature or saturation — to explore a direction quickly.
- Debugging: colour problems are palette problems. Restate the palette instead of regenerating blindly.
