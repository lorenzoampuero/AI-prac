A text-to-image model takes your prompt and **constructs** a picture — it does not look one up. Internally it starts from random noise and refines it step by step toward something that matches your words. The useful part is simpler than the internals: **generation** creates a new image from scratch, while **editing** changes an image you already have — and most tools treat these as separate modes.

## Generation vs editing — two different jobs

- **Generation** fits “make something new”: concepts, drafts, illustrations, mood boards.
- **Editing** fits “change this one thing”: keep the photo, swap the lamp. Tools support editing the whole image with a new prompt, or a selected region only — you mark the area (a mask) and describe the change. [OpenAI’s image API, for example, separates generations from edits exactly this way.](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “Generate the exact photo I saw in that article last month.”
>
> The model has no gallery to search and no memory of your browsing. It will build something plausible instead — and it will not be the photo.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “Here is a photo of our product. Keep everything identical; replace only the background with a light grey studio backdrop and a soft shadow under the object.”
>
> An edit of material you supplied — surgical, verifiable, and exactly what edit modes are for.

## Why it works

Hold two ideas and most image-tool behaviour follows: **generation constructs; editing transforms.** Construction tolerates variety — ask twice, get two samples. Transformation should preserve everything you did not ask to change, which is why dedicated edit modes beat re-describing a whole scene: fewer variables, less accidental drift.

## Transfer

- Product photos: edit backgrounds and light; never regenerate the product itself.
- Iteration: change one description at a time so you can tell what caused what.
- Concepts and mood boards: generate several samples on purpose — variety is the feature here.
