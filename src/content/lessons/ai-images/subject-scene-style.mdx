A strong image prompt fills four slots: **subject** (what is in frame), **scene** (what surrounds it), **style** (how it should look) and **light** (time of day, mood, direction). Naming a thing — “a dog” — leaves three of four slots to chance. Describing all four gives the model independent decisions to make well, and gives you independent dials to turn when you iterate.

## The four slots

1. **Subject** — one clear subject beats three competing ones. Add the telling details: what it is doing, what state it is in.
2. **Scene** — where it sits, what is around it, how busy or calm the setting is.
3. **Style** — a *look*, not praise: “quiet editorial photography”, “flat vector illustration”, “soft watercolor with visible paper grain”.
4. **Light** — direction and mood: “soft morning light through sheer curtains”, “hard noon sun, crisp shadows”, “blue hour, city glow”.

Style words guide when they describe something the model can render. [Generative systems](/glossary/generative-ai) do not reward praise: “stunning” and “amazing” decide nothing.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “A dog.”
>
> Every meaningful decision — setting, style, time of day, mood — is made by the model. You get an image, but not yours.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “A corgi asleep on a windowsill (subject), minimalist room with two plants (scene), soft watercolor illustration with visible paper grain (style), warm late-afternoon light (light).”
>
> Same model, same effort — four deliberate choices instead of none.

## Why it works

The four slots are separable decisions. Filling them all makes the first draft close enough to iterate on, and when something is off you can tell *which slot* caused it. That is how prompt writing becomes engineering instead of guessing.

## Transfer

- Slides and headers: the four slots map one-to-one onto a usable brief.
- Iteration: when something looks wrong, fix the slot responsible — not the whole prompt.
- Briefs for others: the four slots are also a checklist to review someone else’s prompt.
