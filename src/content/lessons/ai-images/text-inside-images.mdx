Ask for a poster and the model will happily draw words — but it draws them as **shapes**, not as typeset letters from a font. That distinction explains everything: lettering often looks plausible while being quietly wrong, and the [current OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) describes text rendering as significantly improved while still listing **precise text placement and clarity** as a limitation. The skill is not avoiding text; it is deciding which words are decoration and which are information.

## What renders well, what wobbles

- **Strong case:** short display words — a few characters, large, bold, high contrast against a calm background. “OPEN”, “SOLE”, a two-word title.
- **Weak case:** anything long or exact — paragraphs of copy, price lists, addresses, URLs, phone numbers, dates. The more characters, the more chances to wobble; the more exactness required, the less plausibility serves you.
- **Stylistic trap:** scripts, handwriting and decorative faces thin out the letterforms — clarity is the first thing they lose.
- **Silent failure:** invented lettering. “COFFE”, “HARVEST FAIRR” — a swapped or doubled letter that reads almost-right at a glance. It is the visual cousin of a [hallucination](/glossary/hallucination): plausible-looking detail that is simply wrong.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “A poster with all the event details: address, website, ticket prices, opening times.”
>
> Four exact strings, none of them large: expect a plausible-looking result with at least one silent error, and no way to fake-check it at a glance.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “A poster with the headline ‘HARVEST FAIR’ in large bold letters at the top, plenty of clear space below.”
>
> The strong case, used on purpose: short and high-contrast in the render, everything exact later in a layout tool.

## Why it works

A [generative system](/glossary/generative-ai) produces letterforms the way it produces leaves: as patterns that match the look of writing, [sampled from noise](/glossary/diffusion) until they fit the prompt. Plausibility is the model's strength; exactness is not. So the durable rule is about stakes, not avoidance: decorative words can tolerate a model's hand, and information-bearing text — names, numbers, addresses — gets typeset by you, in place, afterwards.

## Transfer

- Quote cards and social graphics: one short line in the render is fine — check every character once.
- Posters and menus: headline in the render, body copy and prices in your editor.
- Packaging and labels: treat rendered text as a mockup placeholder; final artwork is typeset.
- Proofreading habit: when a render contains readable text, read it letter by letter — invented lettering is built to pass a glance.
