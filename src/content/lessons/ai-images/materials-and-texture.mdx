A material word tells the model what something is made of; the finish, the wear and the moment decide how it looks. Most weak prompts stop at the material — and the render fills in everything else with the most generic version it knows. Surfaces are where general prompts fail quietly. Skins, fabric, wood, metal: everyone has handled these materials, which means everyone notices when a render gets them wrong. Precision here buys more realism than any other slot.

## Four questions for any surface

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “A luxurious wooden table.”
>
> “Luxurious” is a price tag, not a property. The model picks a generic wood with a generic finish — and nothing in the result is debuggable.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “A thick oak table with an oiled finish, soft sheen, visible grain and gently worn edges; warm side light from the left.”
>
> Every word is a property: species, finish, condition, light. Change one — matt lacquer instead of oil — and the render changes predictably.

## Why it works

Materials are physical claims, and [generative systems](/glossary/generative-ai) render physical claims literally: brushed steel scatters light differently from polished steel because those are different surfaces, not different adjectives. Two extra words — oiled, worn — are often the difference between leather that renders like plastic and leather that looks photographed. The four questions are separable — so debugging a surface is a matter of restating the one answer that went wrong. And because texture only shows under directional light, this lesson stacks on the [lighting fundamentals](/learn/ai-images/lighting-fundamentals): material words describe, light reveals.

## Transfer

- Products: finish words carry perceived quality — matte and satin read premium, gloss reads plastic.
- Interiors: condition words set the story — worn oak and faded linen say “lived in”, pristine surfaces say “showroom”.
- Fabrics: name the weave and drape (chunky knit, crisp cotton, fluid silk), not just the fibre.
- Debugging: when a surface looks wrong, ask which of the four questions went unanswered.
