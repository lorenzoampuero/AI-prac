Camera words are shorthand for **perspective** — where the viewer stands and how that changes what the subject feels like. Used with intent, one or two of them do a lot of work. Stacked without thought, they contradict each other and the model averages noise.

## Angles change meaning

- **Eye level** — the viewer meets the subject as an equal. The neutral default for people.
- **Low angle** (looking up) — the subject feels imposing, powerful, larger than life.
- **High angle** (looking down) — smaller, vulnerable, observed — or practical, like a tabletop.
- **Top-down (flat lay)** — graphic and organised: grids, layouts, food styling from above.

## Lens terms, briefly

- **Wide lens** — more environment, stronger perspective; close up it distorts (bigger nose, curved walls).
- **85mm portrait feel** — compression and flattery: background softly falls away.
- **Macro** — detail so close the subject becomes texture.

One angle + one lens idea is usually plenty. “Low angle, 85mm portrait feel” says something. “Wide-angle close-up portrait with no distortion” says nothing — those words cancel out.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** “Portrait of a chef, wide-angle close-up, no distortion, everything sharp, low angle and top-down.”
>
> Contradictions. The model has to guess which instructions to honour, and the result shows it.

## A better example

> **Ask:** “Eye-level portrait of a chef plating a dish, 85mm feel with a softly blurred kitchen behind, warm light from the left.”
>
> One angle, one lens idea, one light direction — coherent and re-iterable.

## Why it works

Every camera term is a claim about where the viewer is standing and what lens they hold — and [generated images](/glossary/generative-ai) follow those claims the same way they follow scene words. Coherent stacks reinforce each other; contradictory ones dilute each other. Picking one angle and one perspective effect per image keeps the description honest — and lets you debug results by changing a single decision.

## Transfer

- People: eye level is the honest default; go low or high only to say something.
- Food and products: top-down for layout and patterns, low angle for appetite appeal.
- Debugging: if perspective feels wrong, change one camera term at a time.
