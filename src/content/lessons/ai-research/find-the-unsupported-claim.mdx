An [unsupported claim](/glossary/unsupported-claim) is a statement specific enough to be checked, consequential enough to matter — and attached to no evidence. The marker is not tone: fluent text [styles invented sentences](/glossary/hallucination) exactly like true ones. The load falls on you, so learn the shapes that deserve an immediate second look.

## The shapes to catch

- **Precise numbers with no source.** "Grew 23%", "saves 6 hours a week" — a number is a promise of measurement. Where is the measurement?
- **Predictions stated as facts.** "Three vendors will switch pricing by 2028." Nobody knows this; it is a scenario wearing a date.
- **Anonymous authority.** "Experts agree", "studies show", "industry consensus" — which experts, which studies? A claim that names nothing cannot be checked.
- **Superlatives.** "The only solution", "the best approach", "the strongest effect in a decade" — usually someone's framing, not a finding.
- **Specifics about people and organizations.** Roles, titles, dates, quotes — checkable in minutes and frequently wrong.

## The shapes that are fine

- Qualitative generalities: "customers increasingly expect transparency" asserts nothing measurable.
- Statements that name their evidence: "our churn was 4.2% according to the analytics dashboard" — you can open the dashboard.
- Self-limiting lines: "check the official source before acting" sends you somewhere better.

## The three-part test

For each claim, ask: **is it specific? is it consequential? does it name its evidence?** Specific + consequential + no evidence is the danger zone. Specific + consequential + named evidence is a five-second check, not a problem.

## A bad example

> A briefing says: "Our sector grew 23% last year, and experts agree the shift is inevitable."
>
> Two claims, zero sources, one meeting away from becoming the number your leadership quotes for a year.

## A better example

> "The sector grew, per the [industry association's] 2025 report — the 23% figure comes from its page 4 table; the 'inevitable' line is our own framing and should be labeled as such."
>
> Evidence attached, opinion labeled. Both can stay in the deck.

## Transfer

- Inbox triage: scan for digits, dates, names and futures — each needs a source or a label.
- AI-assisted drafts: run the three-part test before anything leaves your hands.
- Your own writing: unsupported claims are contagious — catch them in your sentences too.
