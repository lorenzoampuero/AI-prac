Delegate by consequence: use AI freely where being wrong is cheap and reversible, let it assist where the stakes rise, and keep the decision with a human where the outcome is irreversible or someone has to be accountable. Three tiers, one question each.

## The three tiers

1. **Delegate.** Reversible, low-stakes, verifiable: drafting, summarizing, brainstorming, formatting, transforming. A mistake costs minutes and gets caught by a skim. Most everyday use lives here — enjoy it without ceremony.
2. **Assist.** Real stakes, human decides: research synthesis to be [verified](/learn/ai-research/inspect-the-citations), client-facing drafts to be [reviewed](/learn/ai-for-work/email-that-sounds-like-you), analyses to be checked. AI produces material; you produce the judgment.
3. **Keep human.** Legal, medical and financial decisions about people; safety; anything irreversible; anything with a name on it. AI can still prepare: "list the questions to ask my lawyer" is a perfect request. "Tell me whether I am legally right" is not — a confident verdict is [exactly the wrong output shape](/learn/ai-research/ask-for-uncertainty) when the consequence is real.

## Why the line sits there

Three properties mark the "keep human" zone: **consequence** (how much a mistake costs), **reversibility** (can it be undone), and **accountability** (who signs). The model cannot hold the third one — responsibility does not travel to a language model, whatever its tone suggests. And confident fluency is everywhere: the same mechanism that makes it [make things up](/learn/ai-foundations/why-ai-makes-things-up) — a [hallucination](/glossary/hallucination) in calm prose — also makes it sound right about them.

## The uncomfortable middle

Professionals use AI for exactly these topics — legally, well and daily. The discipline is not avoiding the tool; it is routing the parts correctly:

- **AI → materials**: summaries, question lists, draft language, options with trade-offs.
- **You → the decision**, informed by those materials.
- **A qualified human → the verdict**, whenever the consequence earns one.

## A bad example

> **Ask:** "Am I legally entitled to my full deposit back? Answer yes or no, and write the final reply."
>
> You are asking a system that cannot be accountable for a verdict — and inviting it to sound certain regardless.

## A better example

> **Ask:** "Summarize this letter in three points. List what I should verify under the tenancy rules. Draft a neutral request I can review — and flag anything that reads as legal advice so I check it with an advisor."
>
> AI does the compression and construction; the verdict goes to the professional; the decision and the signature stay with you.

## Transfer

- Health: organize information and questions; the diagnosis belongs to the doctor.
- Money: model scenarios as worksheets; the commitment is yours to make.
- People decisions: hiring, firing, discipline — AI drafts nothing that carries consequences for a person without full human judgment in the loop. Run your [rubric](/glossary/rubric) before anything is said to a person.
