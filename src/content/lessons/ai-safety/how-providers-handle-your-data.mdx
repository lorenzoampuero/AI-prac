Two different questions hide behind "does the AI learn from my chats?" — and only one was answered by [training vs inference](/learn/ai-foundations/training-vs-inference). The **mechanism** question — does chatting change the weights? — has a clean answer: no, [inference](/glossary/inference) reads fixed weights. The **policy** question — what happens to the conversation the product stored? — has a different answer per provider, per plan and per setting. This lesson is about reading that second answer, using one provider's documentation as the worked example (checked 2026-09-23).

## Mechanism is not policy

A message you send lives in several places at once: the context window while the chat is open, the product's stored history, possibly a training pipeline and logs. The weights stay fixed — but the stored text is governed by policy, and policy is where the real privacy question lives. That is why "it does not retrain the model" is true and irrelevant at the same time: the interesting part is the stored conversation, not the [training cutoff](/glossary/training-cutoff).

## What the controls actually control

OpenAI's data-controls page (updated 2026-09-23) documents, among other things:

- **Model training has its own switch** ("Improve the model for everyone"): turn it off and new conversations — and Codex tasks on personal plans — are not used to train OpenAI models. It applies across devices, and signed-out use remembers the choice in that browser.
- **Turning it off does not delete or hide saved chats.** They stay in history until you delete them separately, and archiving a chat does not change its training eligibility.
- **Temporary chats** stay out of history and memories, are not used to improve models — and may still be retained for up to 30 days for safety.
- **Managed workspaces** (Business, Enterprise, Edu, Healthcare) are not used for training by default; the organization's retention and access policies govern instead.
- **Feedback is a loophole worth knowing**: rating a response opens the possibility that the whole associated conversation is used for training — even after opting out.
- **Memory and training are separate settings.** Turning off memory does not turn off training.

That list is one provider on one date — the point is not to memorise it. When the page changes, the summary expires; the durable skill is asking the right five questions of any provider.

## A bad example

> **Summary:** "We turned off training, so our old chats were deleted for safety — nothing to worry about."
>
> Two false claims in one sentence. Training off does not delete saved chats, and deletion is always a separate action. The reassurance is exactly backwards.

## A better example

> **Summary:** "Per the provider's data-controls page (checked today): new conversations are excluded from training after the opt-out; saved chats remain until deleted separately; temporary chats may be kept up to 30 days for safety. Not covered there: the retention window for our plan."
>
> Now the claim has a source, a date and an explicit boundary — something a reviewer can check instead of trust.

## Transfer

- Before pasting something sensitive, check three things: personal or workspace account, training setting, retention window.
- One-off sensitive questions: temporary chat — remembering the 30-day safety retention and the personalization choice at the start.
- Do not rate with thumbs what you would not want stored: feedback can opt a conversation in.
- Keep the date with every policy claim, this lesson included; re-read the page when the product changes. The [redaction](/glossary/redaction) habit from earlier lessons still does the heavy lifting.
