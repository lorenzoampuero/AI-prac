Using AI at work well comes down to a plan you write before you start: **what you paste, what never, what you verify, what you disclose**. Four lines, reused for every task. The plan is personal, but it answers to three masters: your employer's rules, your readers' trust, and your own name.

## The four lines

1. **Paste** — the [classified](/glossary/data-classification)-safe version: scrubbed data, public material, your own text. [What not to paste](/learn/ai-safety/what-not-to-paste) set the buckets; the plan names them once so you stop re-deciding per task.
2. **Never** — secrets, personal data, confidential figures, and untrusted content with a tool-enabled assistant (the [prompt-injection](/glossary/prompt-injection) boundary, drilled in [the injection lesson](/learn/ai-safety/prompt-injection-simply)).
3. **Verify** — every number, name, quote and claim against its source before it appears in anything sent or published. Treat output as a draft with your name pending — [the five-question check](/learn/prompting/evaluate-before-you-ship) is the standing pass.
4. **Disclose** — follow your workplace's rules: some require labeling AI-assisted content, some restrict it for certain tasks entirely. When no policy exists, say so and set a local norm with your team — the absence of a rule is not a license; it is a gap you just found.

## The meeting test

Before AI-assisted output goes into something consequential, three questions:

- Can I explain where each fact came from?
- Can I defend this text if someone questions it?
- Would I be comfortable if the whole process were visible?

Three yeses — ship it. Any no — fix that step first. The test is quick, and it encodes everything above in one pass.

## Judgment calls that recur

- **Tone about people**: AI drafts, you decide — reviewing performance, saying no, delicate news. The draft can help; the choice is yours.
- **Policy gray zones**: ask early, not after. One message to your manager beats one embarrassed meeting.
- **Your accountability**: whatever the tool contributed, the signature is human. That is not a formality — it is why the other three lines exist.

## Worked example

> **Task:** a quarterly report section with customer examples.
> **Paste:** the outline, my own paragraphs, dashboard *structure* with fake sample values.
> **Never:** customer names and contract figures, raw exports, unreleased numbers.
> **Verify:** every figure against its dashboard; every example fictionalized or cleared; numbers re-typed, not copied.
> **Disclose:** change-log note that AI assisted the draft, plus a flag to the manager that no team policy exists yet.

## Transfer

- New job, new tool: rewrite the four lines for that context in ten minutes.
- Freelance or client work: version of the plan per client, agreed in writing.
- Teams: share your plan as a starting point — local norms beat silent variation.
